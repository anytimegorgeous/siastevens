27 Green Flags in a Relationship, and Why They’re Grown, Not Found

We all know the red-flag checklist by heart. Almost nobody asks where the flags come from, why the same person shows green to one partner and red to the next, or how to grow more green.

By Sia StevensNovember 6, 20267 min read

A while ago I was asked to give a talk on a topic you already know by heart: how to avoid red flags in relationships. So I sat down and made the list. Lying. Cheating. Gaslighting. Manipulation. And of course narcissism, the red flag to end all red flags.

Then I looked back at my own relationships and noticed something strange. None of those red flags had been visible at the beginning. Not one. In the beginning it was all green: a big, bright, perfectly mowed green lawn. And then, slowly, like weeds coming up through the grass, the red started showing.

Flags aren’t absolutes

We like to believe green flags belong to good people and red flags belong to bad ones. But if that were true, people couldn’t switch colors so easily, and they do, constantly. The person who was cold and careless in one relationship becomes kind, patient, even romantic in the next. The one who barely acknowledged you is cooking dinner every night for somebody new.

We all carry both red and green flags within us. What brings one color out over the other isn’t luck, astrology, or finding “the one.” It’s the conditions we create around each other.

That changes the whole project. A red-flag checklist only helps you screen. It can’t help you build. And everyone already has that list, while the garden kept decaying anyway. The more useful question is the other half nobody teaches: how do you create the conditions that bring out the best in someone, including yourself?

The 27 green flags

A Million Green Flags ends every chapter with one green flag: one thing to look for in someone, and one thing to become yourself. Twenty-seven chapters, twenty-seven flags. Three stages, compounded over themselves: three times three times three. Here is the whole book as a checklist.

  1. They believe a healthy relationship is something you build, not something you find.
  2. They put the phone face-down when you start talking — without being asked.
  3. "Let’s watch it together" — and they actually wait for you.
  4. They close their other options on purpose. They chose you, and stopped shopping.
  5. They apply one thing they’ve learned instead of quoting ten things they’ve read.
  6. Their interest grows as they see the real you — tired, wrong, ordinary.
  7. They’re the same person in every room — with you, with their friends, with the waiter.
  8. They have at least one old friendship that survived being boring.
  9. When you mess up, they try to understand you before they judge you.
  10. They’re constantly building their life — putting their time and energy to good use.
  11. They reach back: they initiate too, they match your effort, and they repair after fights without being pushed.
  12. Their needs run through people, not services — their life would still hold if the paycheck stopped.
  13. They enter your world without being invited — they read your book, they show up at your race.
  14. They let you see their needs, and let you help — no shame, no scorekeeping. "I could do this myself — but I’d rather do it with you."
  15. Their affection shows up on ordinary Tuesdays, not just on special occasions.
  16. Somewhere in their life is something they’ve tended consistently for years — they’ve built more than they’ve consumed.
  17. They defend your small rituals — and they’re always proposing something to build together. "Our thing" is a real reason; "someday" becomes a plan.
  18. They have a village — and there’s already a chair in it for you.
  19. They can argue without breaking — fights end with repair, not with silence.
  20. They make you better, not smaller — around them, you become your green flags.
  21. They keep life deliberately simple — fewer options, fewer distractions, chosen on purpose — and it shows: they’re focused and decisive.
  22. They make you feel like the only person in the room.
  23. They notice your effort and name it — specifically, without being prompted.
  24. They resist shortcuts. They work for things rather than expecting them to arrive automatically.
  25. They state their standards early and warmly — and then actually live by them.
  26. They rise to your standards.
  27. They become all of them, starting with the one you’ll be today.

Read it twice. The first time, think of someone you’re with or interested in. The second time, read it about yourself. That second reading is the one that changes things. You can tick them off here, “in them” and “in me.”

Why the list starts with building, and ends with becoming

Look at the first flag: they believe a healthy relationship is something you build, not something you find. Almost every flag after it is a version of the same idea, applied to a different stage of a relationship. Attention that doesn’t wander to the phone. Effort that still shows up on an ordinary Tuesday. A life that runs through people, not only services. Fights that end in repair instead of silence.

And the last flag isn’t about them at all: they become all of them, starting with the one you’ll be today. That’s the point of the whole list. Flags were never labels. They’re seeds. Both colors, in everyone, waiting on conditions.

How to grow green flags

  • Pay attention. Attention is the currency of love, and for the first time in history a trillion-dollar economy is competing with the people you love for yours.
  • Reward what you want more of. You are your partner’s reward structure. What you notice and honor grows. What you ignore fades.
  • Hold your standards, calmly. State them early and warmly, then live by them. A line you’d drop for a big enough offer was never a line. It was a price.

Pay attention, give freely, hold your standards, and the green comes out on its own, the way a seed given light and water doesn’t strain to flower.

The 27 green flags are the closing list of A Million Green Flags; the opening of this piece is from Chapter 1, “Where Are the Green Flags?”
