The Fable · Fiction

What Happened to Us

A Fable of Modern Love

It ended on a Tuesday, over takeout they didn’t finish. No fight. No betrayal. Just a good man in her doorway saying: nothing happened. I just don’t feel it anymore, and I don’t know why.

Sia debugs code for a living, and she cannot get over the fact that her own love story ended with no known root cause. So she goes looking — from Seattle’s streets to snow-covered peaks, from a Hollywood vineyard to a Himalayan monastery, to a Greek village kitchen where she is handed the wisdom our villages knew and our cities deleted.

What she brings home is bigger than an answer: an engineer’s framework of modern love, delivered on a stage before a thousand women, with the two men she loved in the crowd.

The Manual · Nonfiction

A Million Green Flags

How to Build Lasting Relationships in the Digital Age

We are the most connected generation in history — and the loneliest ever measured. The people didn’t get worse. The conditions did. The ghosting, the breadcrumbing, the games — these aren’t character flaws. They’re symptoms of a starving generation, in a world engineered to keep it hungry.

Drawing on psychology and behavioral economics, with an activity at the end of every chapter, this is the manual nobody handed you: how attraction sparks, how connection sustains, how love lasts, and how relationships transform.

It won’t teach you to avoid red flags. It teaches something better: how to grow the green ones.

The Framework

Every lasting relationship moves through three stages.

Attention and value are the two raw materials every connection is made of. Each stage is just those two doing different work — and the stages are a cycle, not a staircase. When the cycle keeps turning, the relationship lives. When it stops, you get the sentence everyone says: nothing happened, it just faded.

01

Attraction

fuel: value

Something draws you in — you notice a person and recognize their value. Not magic. A measurement: a live reading of perceived value, rising, falling, buildable, rebuildable.

02

Reciprocation

fuel: exchange

You invest. Attention becomes engagement, value gets exchanged back and forth, and trust accumulates in the flow. The ordinary Tuesday is the relationship.

03

Co-creation

fuel: growth

You build. Attention becomes shared focus, and the two of you create value that didn’t exist before either of you: rituals, a home, a future tense. Love consumed burns down. Love built compounds.

“Red flags and green flags are two directions the same force can point — and conditions decide which one grows.”The fable walks the three stages as a story. The manual explains each one, and hands you the labors.

About the Author

Sia Stevens

Sia Stevens is a writer and relationship educator. Before either of those, she was a software engineer — a decade building systems at scale — and then an MBA, studying how incentives shape what people do. Somewhere along the way, the question changed: from how systems fail to why the most important system of all — the way we love — quietly fails the people running it.

A breakup she couldn’t explain sent her looking for the root cause of love itself. The year-long search became What Happened to Us, a fable; the framework it arrives at — attraction, reciprocation, co-creation — became A Million Green Flags, and the course Modern Relationships 101. She publishes under her own imprint, Vessel & Ink Press, and founded Alt Therapy.

She lives in Seattle, in sight of Mount Rainier.
